\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}

Virtual cycling combines smart trainers, route simulation and physiological
analysis, yet the cyclist's workflow is often fragmented across route, training
and analysis applications. This work presents Argus Cyclist, an open-source,
cross-platform software ecosystem for simulating, exploring and analysing real
routes in virtual cycling. The thesis guiding the project is that a single
workflow serves riders preparing for a race who want to recon the course, riders
who want to experience a place they may never visit in person, and riders who
want to train with physiological data. The GPX file is imported, the trainer
simulates the road, telemetry is analysed and, on desktop, the history remains
on the device; on Android, the live session covers import, simulation and
telemetry, without FIT or history. The desktop was developed in Go, with a
JavaScript frontend embedded through Wails; the same frontend is reused on
Android through Capacitor, where physics and GPX parsing are reimplemented in
JavaScript. The project follows Clean Architecture principles, Facade and
Repository patterns, and local SQLite persistence on desktop. The desktop
physics engine uses Martin's equation to estimate speed from gradient; the
desktop analysis module computes Normalized Power (NP), Intensity Factor (IF),
Training Stress Score (TSS), Training Impulse (TRIMP), the Performance
Management Chart (PMC), heart rate recovery (HRR) and aerobic decoupling, with
documented formulas and limitations. The study adopts Design Science Research
and evaluates the artifact through a pilot study with the author, complemented
by integration and \texttt{.FIT} export tests. Windows/Linux desktop (full
workflow) and an Android phone (live session) were validated; iOS/iPadOS, an
Android tablet and macOS desktop remain architectural preparation or projected
layout, without device validation. The code is released
under GPLv3, and the initial version was registered with the Brazilian National
Institute of Industrial Property (INPI, Process BR512026003786-7).

\textbf{Keywords}: Virtual cycling. Real routes. Open source. Physiological
analysis. Cross-platform. Design Science Research.

\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
